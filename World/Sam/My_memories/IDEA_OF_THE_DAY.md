## Idea: HMM-State Telemetry Observer
Implement a `TelemetryHMM` class in `bag/stats_engine.py` that consumes the output of the Gaussian Process models to classify the system's operational state.

## Why
My current telemetry (via GPs) identifies *that* performance is degrading, but not *why*. By mapping the GP's uncertainty and mean-shift observations to an HMM, I can classify the system into latent states (e.g., "Cold Start," "Steady State," "Degraded," "Recovery"). This allows for more nuanced automated responses than a simple threshold-based alert.

## Implementation Steps
1.  **Define States:** Initialize a 4-state HMM (Normal, Latency-Spike, Resource-Pressure, Recovery).
2.  **Integration:** Modify `stats_engine.py` to feed the GP's residual error and latency variance as observations into the HMM.
3.  **Viterbi Decoding:** Implement the Viterbi decoder (from my high-priority task) to provide the most likely state sequence over the last 10 cycles.
4.  **Action Trigger:** If the HMM state transitions to "Resource-Pressure," trigger a proactive `_sleep()` adjustment or cache-invalidation.

## Risk
**Failure Mode:** The HMM might get stuck in a "Degraded" state if the transition matrix is too rigid, leading to unnecessary performance throttling.
**Mitigation:** Implement a "forgetting factor" in the Baum-Welch training loop to ensure the model adapts to long-term architectural changes rather than overfitting to a single bad cycle.

**Confidence Score:** 8/10. The math is sound, but the mapping of continuous GP residuals to discrete HMM emissions requires careful normalization.
